pg 01 — the honest part
The beginning

I failed 10th. Then I failed Inter.

Both times, money was the real reason. For the final exams we hadn't paid the fee. We went and asked, and somehow got a hall ticket. I retried. Passed.

But the certificate is still sitting in that college office. We never had the ₹3,000 to collect it. No degree, then. Not for lack of trying. Collecting your own results costs money too, and we didn't have it.

The nudge

My brother said it like it was obvious: "Just try YouTube. What else are you doing?"

So I did.

▶ watch that first video bad lighting, zero plan, posted anyway
Four years. Not one rupee.

I just kept uploading. Whatever felt right that day. 0 views. 3 views. 7 on a good week.

Paid for a content coach once. He said things I already knew, in a more confident voice. Tried every "grow fast" trick I could find. Bought fake subscribers once, out of desperation, and watched a number go up that meant nothing.

Four years. Not a single rupee came in.

What actually changed

It wasn't posting more. I was already doing that. It wasn't working harder.

It was one thing: understanding why someone watches a video till the end, and then deciding that on purpose, before filming.

pg 06 — everything else

Here's how I'd think about your situation.

Not a course. Just what I'd actually do. Pick the one that sounds like you.

Don't think about growing yet. Just post something that's actually yours. Mine was IKEA bags into trousers. It was weird. That was the point.
Most likely you're posting things that someone else already said better. I did that for months. The fix wasn't trying harder. It was stopping the copying.
That one hit worked for a reason. Find it. Not the topic: the structure, the hook, the feeling it created. That thing is repeatable. Everything else was noise.
I started with exactly ₹20,000. The trick wasn't the money. It was documenting everything, publicly, from day one.
